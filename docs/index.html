Why DrivePass is different

🚫

No server to breach

Every major password manager with a central server is a target. DrivePass has none — your encrypted vault lives in a Drive account you already own and protect.

🔐

Real zero-knowledge crypto

PBKDF2-SHA256 (210,000 iterations) derives an AES-256-GCM key from your master password, on your device. Google sees only ciphertext. We see nothing at all.

📂

Only one file, nothing else

DrivePass uses Google's narrowest Drive permission (drive.file): it can access only the single vault.enc file it created — not your documents, photos, or anything else.

⚡

Everything you expect

Autofill, save-login prompts, a strong password generator, search, auto-lock, and sync across your devices through your own Drive.

🛡️

Security Center

Find reused, weak, old, and breached passwords. Health checks run 100% locally; the optional breach check uses anonymous k-anonymity hashes.

💸

Free, honestly funded

No ads, no data sales, no paywall. DrivePass is funded by clearly-marked partner recommendations in the Security Center that open only if you click them.

How it works

  1. Create a master password. It never leaves your device — it isn't stored anywhere, ever.
  2. Your vault is encrypted locally into a single file, vault.enc.
  3. Connect Google Drive (optional) and that one encrypted file syncs across your devices.
  4. Forget the master password and nobody can recover the vault — including us. That's the point.
Remember the LastPass breaches? When there's no vault server, there's no vault-server breach.
